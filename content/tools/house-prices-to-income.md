$$
\text{Price to household income}_t = \frac{\text{nominal house price index}_t}{\text{household disposable income per capita}_t}
$$

The numerator is the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development nominal house price index for Australia, 2015 = 100. The denominator is Australian Bureau of Statistics household gross disposable income, seasonally adjusted, divided by estimated resident population. The income is quarterly and in dollars per person.

The level is index points per dollar of quarterly disposable income per capita. Read it against its own history: the shape shows whether housing is stretched relative to the income households actually have to spend.

The denominator is household disposable income, not average weekly earnings. It includes rent received by household landlords and excludes corporate, institutional and foreign investors' income. It is the standard affordability denominator used by the OECD price-to-income ratio.

The quartile lines mark the 25th and 75th percentiles of the selected range, so the current reading can be read against its own recent distribution.

**Data Sources**

- Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development, nominal house price index for Australia
- Australian Bureau of Statistics, 5206.0 Table 20, household gross disposable income (seasonally adjusted)
- Australian Bureau of Statistics, estimated resident population
- Frequency: quarterly
